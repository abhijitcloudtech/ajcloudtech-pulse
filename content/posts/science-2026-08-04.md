## Cosmic Anomaly Revealed: Asteroid (44) Nysa Found with Three Lobes and a Moon

**August 04, 2026** – The solar system continues to unveil its secrets, with astronomers today announcing the discovery of an asteroid unlike any previously observed. New images of (44) Nysa, located in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, reveal a unique three-lobed structure and a previously unknown small moon. This fascinating discovery, published today, offers an unprecedented glimpse into the complex history of celestial bodies.

Using data from advanced telescopes, including the Large Binocular Telescope on Mount Graham in Arizona, an international research team captured detailed features of (44) Nysa's surface. Researchers first spotted (44) Nysa in 1857, noting it as one of the brightest visible asteroids. While initial observations hinted at an elongated or possibly bilobate (two-lobed) shape, the recent high-resolution images have confirmed a distinct three-lobed formation. These lobes are connected by "colli," neck-like features that form between separate segments of the asteroid.

Adding to the asteroid's intrigue, the new images also unveiled a small, previously unknown moon, now named S/2026 (44)1. This moon is estimated to be just over half a mile in diameter, orbiting its much larger parent at a distance of approximately 100 miles. By analyzing the moon's velocity and orbit, scientists hope to more precisely calculate (44) Nysa's mass and density, offering further clues about its formation and evolution. This discovery not only enhances our understanding of asteroid morphologies but also highlights the dynamic and often surprising nature of our cosmic neighborhood.
