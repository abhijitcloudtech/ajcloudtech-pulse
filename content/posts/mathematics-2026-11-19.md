### Fields Medals 2026: Celebrating Mathematical Brilliance

Today, November 19, 2026, we look back at one of the most significant events in the world of mathematics this year: the awarding of the prestigious Fields Medals. Often regarded as the "Nobel Prize of Mathematics," these medals recognize outstanding mathematical achievement for existing work and the promise of future breakthroughs by mathematicians under 40 years of age. The 2026 awards, presented in July, highlighted the remarkable depth and diversity of contemporary mathematical research.

This year, four brilliant minds were honored for their transformative contributions to various fields of mathematics:

*   **Yu Deng** of the University of Chicago was recognized for his groundbreaking work in partial differential equations, including rigorous derivations for the Boltzmann equation and contributions to nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics.
*   **John Pardon** from Stony Brook University received the medal for his significant achievements in symplectic geometry, encompassing new approaches to virtual fundamental cycles, Fukaya categories, and knot theory.
*   **Jacob Tsimerman** of the University of Toronto was celebrated for his vast extension of o-minimal techniques within arithmetic and complex algebraic geometry, notably proving Griffiths' conjecture on the algebraicity of images of period maps.
*   **Hong Wang** from New York University and Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques earned recognition for her impactful work in harmonic analysis and geometric measure theory, including advances in the local smoothing conjecture and the Kakeya problem in three dimensions. Wang is the third woman to receive the Fields Medal.

These laureates exemplify the creativity, rigor, and profound impact of mathematical research in solving complex problems and opening new avenues of inquiry. Their work continues to push the boundaries of human knowledge and inspire future generations of mathematicians.
