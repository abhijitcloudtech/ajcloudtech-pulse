### The Algorithmic Ascent: AI Takes Center Stage in Mathematical Discovery

**November 16, 2026** – While daily headlines often focus on politics or technology, the world of mathematics has been witnessing its own quiet revolution, increasingly driven by artificial intelligence. Today, mathematical breakthroughs are not just the product of lone genius but also of sophisticated algorithms and collaborative AI systems, fundamentally reshaping how new truths are uncovered and verified.

Just this week, on November 16, 2026, the Justin Sun Prize recognized significant advancements in solving Erdős problems, explicitly highlighting the role of AI assistance and Lean formalization in the awarded work on Erdős Problems #97 and #841. This follows a broader trend where AI is proving its mettle in tackling long-standing mathematical puzzles. In April 2026, an AI model, GPT-5.4, famously solved an "asymptotic" version of the Erdős Primitive Set Conjecture (Erdős Problem #1196) in approximately 80 minutes, with the resulting proof lauded as a "Book Proof" for its elegance. Earlier in February 2026, Google DeepMind's Gemini model also demonstrated its capability by proving four of the 700 open Erdős problems.

The rapid acceleration in AI's mathematical prowess is evident, with AI systems achieving gold-medal level performance in International Mathematical Olympiad challenges by 2025-2026. Beyond problem-solving, AI is also revolutionizing the verification of mathematical proofs. Formal verification tools, such as Lean and Coq, are gaining prominence, with some leading mathematics journals reportedly requiring formalization for computationally intensive proofs as of 2026. This push for formalization ensures rigor and helps validate complex, often machine-generated, results. Even historical mathematical work is being re-examined with AI; an AI model, Claude, has been instrumental in extracting and verifying theorems from Kurt Gödel's notebooks, uncovering previously overlooked insights.

While the September 2026 announcement by OpenAI regarding AI agents identifying a "singularity" in the Navier-Stokes equations sparked considerable discussion, with ongoing expert evaluation distinguishing the scope of this achievement from the full Millennium Prize Problem, the overall trajectory is clear: AI is an indispensable tool in modern mathematical research.

The collaboration between human intuition and artificial intelligence is forging a new path for mathematical discovery. This symbiotic relationship promises to unlock deeper understandings and solve problems that have eluded mathematicians for generations.
