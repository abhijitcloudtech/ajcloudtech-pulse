## Breakthrough in Protein Crystallization: DNA Unlocks New Era for Biomaterials

**July 30, 2026** – A significant advancement in chemistry reported today by Northwestern University is set to revolutionize how we understand proteins and design next-generation biomaterials. For decades, coaxing proteins into crystalline forms – a crucial step for determining their molecular structures and designing drugs – has been a painstaking, unpredictable, trial-and-error process. Now, chemists have introduced an ingenious approach that replaces this uncertainty with intentional design.

The breakthrough involves repurposing flexible DNA strands not just as genetic material, but as a blueprint and programmable molecular glue. By leveraging DNA, the research team successfully directed proteins to assemble into diffraction-quality crystal structures with unprecedented atomic-level order. This novel strategy allows for precise control over how proteins connect, leading to the creation of unusually soft and flexible crystals while maintaining the high structural integrity essential for detailed analysis.

This innovation dramatically simplifies one of structural biology's most challenging hurdles. It not only offers a more efficient pathway for uncovering protein structures, which are vital blueprints for drug development and enzyme engineering, but also overturns the long-held assumption that flexible building blocks cannot achieve such high structural order. The implications extend to the development of novel flexible biomaterials with tailored properties, opening new avenues in medicine and materials science.
