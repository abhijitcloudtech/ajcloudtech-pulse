## AI Unlocks Decades-Old Math Mysteries: OpenAI's Astra Delivers 10 New Proofs

**August 03, 2026** – The world of mathematics is abuzz with the groundbreaking announcement from OpenAI, whose new "Astra" model has reportedly solved ten long-standing mathematical problems for a mere $2,000. Published on August 1st, this marks a significant leap in AI's role in mathematical discovery, with machine-checkable proofs accompanying the solutions.

The results span diverse fields including group theory, high-dimensional geometry, coding theory, and extremal combinatorics, arriving as a 249-page manuscript collection complete with machine-checkable certificates for every single result. These proofs were formalized in Lean, a proof assistant that verifies mathematical arguments step by step, allowing for instant and trustless verification. This approach fundamentally alters the traditional peer review process and addresses a key challenge in AI deployment by providing verifiable outputs.

This breakthrough highlights a potential shift in mathematical research, where the cost of discovery could be dramatically reduced, and the ability to guarantee correctness becomes paramount. While questions remain regarding problem selection and model access, the verifiable nature of Astra's work signifies a major step for scientific reasoning and the evolving collaboration between human mathematicians and artificial intelligence.

The mathematical community is engaging with these developments, with some expressing both excitement and caution about the impact of AI. This event follows earlier news from May 2026, where another OpenAI reasoning model disproved a longstanding conjectured bound in Paul Erdős's planar unit-distance problem, further underscoring AI's growing prowess in tackling complex mathematical challenges.
