## Cosmic Baby Breaks Records: Astronomers Discover Youngest Exoplanet Ever

**September 19, 2026** – Science continues its relentless march forward, pushing the boundaries of human understanding. This week, the cosmos delivered a truly remarkable piece of news: astronomers have confirmed the existence of Elias 2-24 b, the youngest exoplanet ever discovered, offering an unprecedented glimpse into the very birth of a world.

At less than a million years old, this Jupiter-sized "baby planet" is still actively gathering material from the swirling disk of gas and dust surrounding its young host star, Elias 2-24, located approximately 450 light-years from Earth. The discovery of Elias 2-24 b, which is between two and four times as massive as Jupiter and almost twice as far from its home star as Neptune is from our Sun, provides scientists with a rare opportunity to study a giant planet while it is still in its formative stages.

This groundbreaking finding, combining observations from the W. M. Keck Observatory, the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), and the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope (VLT), significantly challenges existing theories on how giant planets coalesce, particularly those forming rapidly at vast distances from their stars. For years, astronomers had debated whether a faint point of light within a gap observed in the star's dusty disk could indeed be a planet, as current models suggested a large planet shouldn't form so quickly. The confirmation of Elias 2-24 b is a testament to advanced data-processing techniques and the power of archival astronomical data.

In other significant recent news, a new study has revised our understanding of Earth's origins. Published on September 16, 2026, research suggests that our planet formed almost entirely from material in the inner Solar System, challenging the long-held notion that water-rich material from beyond Jupiter played a major role. This implies that Earth's water may have been present much closer to the young Sun than scientists previously believed.

The confirmation of Elias 2-24 b and the re-evaluation of Earth's formation highlight the dynamic nature of scientific inquiry and the continuous evolution of our cosmic narrative.
