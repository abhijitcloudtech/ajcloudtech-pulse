## HR's Shifting Sands: Key Trends Defining July 2026

The world of Human Resources continues its rapid evolution, with July 2026 bringing several critical developments that underscore HR's increasingly strategic and complex role. From landmark regulatory changes to the pervasive influence of artificial intelligence, HR leaders are navigating a dynamic landscape that demands agility and a human-centric approach.

One of the most significant pieces of actual news this month is the **new federal guidance on AI in HR decision-making**, effective July 1, 2026. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and the Department of Justice (DOJ) have issued directives emphasizing the need for employers to conduct bias audits, ensure transparency in AI use, and provide reasonable accommodations for individuals with disabilities interacting with AI systems. This highlights a growing imperative for ethical AI deployment, especially as workers express concerns about a "loss of humanity" in the workplace due to AI's prevalence.

Concurrently, employee support and compliance are seeing pivotal shifts. The **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) definitions have expanded**, also effective July 1, 2026. The Department of Labor (DOL) has broadened the definition of "family member" to explicitly include domestic partners, grandparents, and any individual for whom the employee stands in loco parentis, reflecting modern family structures and caregiving responsibilities. This change requires HR departments to update policies and ensure broader support for employees. In another notable development, the EEOC has submitted a plan to potentially eliminate EEO-1 reporting requirements for private employers, though the impact on 2025 reports remains to be clarified.

Amidst these regulatory updates, the workforce itself remains in flux. Layoffs, particularly in the tech sector, continue at a significant pace. By July 1, 2026, AI-related layoffs alone had reached 128,000, indicating a substantial restructuring of roles as technology advances. This underscores the ongoing importance of a **skills-based approach** to talent management, as organizations increasingly prioritize capabilities over traditional job titles to build adaptable workforces. HR's role is shifting from a support function to a strategic operator, focused on driving measurable business outcomes while championing employee wellbeing and retention.
