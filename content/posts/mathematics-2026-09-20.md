## Mathematics World Celebrates Fields Medalists at ICM 2026

The mathematical community buzzed with excitement this July as the prestigious Fields Medals for 2026 were awarded at the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM) in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. This quadrennial event, often considered the "Nobel Prize of mathematics," recognized four exceptional young mathematicians for their groundbreaking work.

The International Mathematical Union (IMU) honored Yu Deng of the University of Chicago, John Pardon of Stony Brook University, Jacob Tsimerman of the University of Toronto, and Hong Wang of New York University and France's Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques (IHES). These brilliant minds, all under 40, were celebrated for their significant achievements that are shaping the future of mathematics.

Yu Deng was cited for his work in partial differential equations, including a rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation. John Pardon received his medal for contributions to topology and geometry. Jacob Tsimerman was recognized for his role in extending o-minimal techniques within arithmetic and complex algebraic geometry, notably proving Griffiths' conjecture. Hong Wang, notably the third woman ever to win a Fields Medal, was honored for her profound work in analysis and geometric measure theory, including the full proof of the three-dimensional Kakeya conjecture.

The Fields Medal highlights not only outstanding achievement but also the promise of future breakthroughs, inspiring generations of mathematicians worldwide.
