## Frog Bacterium Offers New Hope in Cancer Fight

**August 06, 2026**

In a groundbreaking development that could reshape cancer treatment, scientists have identified a naturally occurring bacterium from amphibian intestines capable of completely eliminating colorectal tumors in mice with a single treatment. This remarkable discovery, published in *Gut Microbes* on July 10, 2026, by researchers from the Japan Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (JAIST), introduces a promising new avenue for targeted cancer therapy.

The bacterium, identified as *Ewingella americana*, was isolated from the intestines of Japanese tree frogs (*Dryophytes japonicus*). Unlike previous approaches that focused on altering the gut microbiome, this research centered on isolating specific bacterial strains, growing them in the laboratory, and delivering them intravenously to directly combat tumors.

In mouse models of colorectal cancer, a single intravenous dose of *E. americana* achieved a 100% complete response rate, entirely eliminating tumors. This powerful effect is attributed to the bacterium's dual mechanism of action: it directly attacks cancer cells while also activating the host's immune system to fight the disease.

The findings represent a significant leap forward in the quest for novel cancer therapies and suggest that living bacteria could one day form the basis of treatments for various solid tumors.
