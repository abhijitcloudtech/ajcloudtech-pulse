## New Solar Images Offer Clues to the Sun's Enduring Coronal Mystery

**August 05, 2026** – Astronomers have today unveiled unprecedentedly detailed images of the Sun's surface, providing crucial insights into one of solar physics' longest-standing puzzles: why the Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona, is millions of degrees hotter than its surface.

The images, captured by the powerful Inouye Solar Telescope in Hawaii, reveal intricate features, including golden bands and swirling vortices, on the turbulent solar surface. These newly observed structures are smaller than 20 kilometers across and were found in a magnetically active region near a sunspot.

Scientists believe these high-resolution observations of small, whirlpool-like structures are signatures of processes that transfer energy from the Sun's surface into its corona. This energy transfer mechanism is thought to be responsible for heating the corona to extreme temperatures, a phenomenon that has baffled researchers for decades. This breakthrough marks a significant step towards understanding our star's fundamental physics and could enhance our predictions of space weather, which impacts technology on Earth.
