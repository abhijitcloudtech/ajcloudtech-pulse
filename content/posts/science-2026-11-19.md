## Voyager 1 Reaches One Light-Day from Earth: A Historic Milestone in Interstellar Exploration

**November 19, 2026** – Humanity’s most distant ambassador, NASA's Voyager 1 probe, has achieved another monumental milestone, officially reaching one light-day from Earth on November 18, 2026. This means the venerable spacecraft is now so far away that it takes light a full 24 hours to travel from the probe to our home planet.

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 embarked on a "Grand Tour" of the outer planets, revolutionizing our understanding of Jupiter and Saturn. In 2012, it made history by becoming the first human-made object to enter interstellar space, the vast region between star systems. Its twin, Voyager 2, followed suit in 2018.

The probe, having traveled for over 49 years, continues to send back invaluable data, even as its signals take an entire day to reach the Deep Space Network antennas on Earth. This unprecedented journey provides scientists with direct measurements of the interstellar environment, offering insights into the heliosphere – the protective bubble of particles and magnetic fields created by our Sun – and the cosmic radiation beyond it. While mission engineers continue to analyze an issue impacting science and engineering data since November 14, the probe's carrier signal confirmed this latest distance record.

The ongoing mission of Voyager 1 underscores the incredible longevity and enduring scientific value of these pioneering spacecraft, which continue to expand our cosmic horizons.
